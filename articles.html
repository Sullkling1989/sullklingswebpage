Snars-A New Discovery

Introduction

Recent domestic observations have led to the identification of a previously undocumented organism informally designated The Snar. The organism appears to exhibit unusual electro-parasitic behavior, feeding directly on household electrical systems and low-voltage computer components. This paper summarizes the initial observations, containment methods, and preliminary behavioral experiments conducted on a small population discovered within a personal desktop computer.

The Discovery Of The Organism

The discovery occurred during what initially appeared to be a routine hardware malfunction. Over a period of approximately 72 hours, my personal computer began exhibiting unusual symptoms including repeated system crashes, excessive thermal output (average internal case temperature rising from 41°C to 83°C under minimal load), and spontaneous power fluctuations. Initial diagnostic tests suggested severe electrical instability. Hardware components—including the power supply unit and motherboard voltage regulators—eventually failed due to what appeared to be sustained electrical drain.

Upon disassembling the computer case to investigate the damage, I discovered several small, previously unknown organisms clustered near the power supply cables and GPU power connectors. These creatures appeared to be feeding directly on electrical current, attaching themselves to conductive surfaces in a manner somewhat analogous to leeches.

Containment Proceedure

Immediate containment became necessary once the population within the computer case was confirmed. Due to the lack of specialized laboratory equipment, I conducted a series of improvised containment trials using common household containers.
  • Three containers were tested:
  • Glass jar (0.8 L capacity)
  • Food storage container (polypropylene, 1.2 L)
  • 1 L Tesco plastic juice jug
Unexpectedly, the Tesco 1 L plastic jug proved significantly more effective. The Snars displayed a strong preference for the jug’s material and geometry, settling along the interior walls within 12–18 seconds of placement inside.Preliminary hypotheses suggest the following factors may contribute: The polyethylene density (~0.94 g/cm³) may approximate the natural nesting surfaces preferred by Snars. The curved cylindrical shape may mimic the topology of electrical cable bundles. Residual orange juice sugars may have provided trace electrolytes, increasing surface conductivity.

Population Survey

Following successful containment of the first group, a wider inspection of the household electrical system was conducted. Additional Snars were discovered in several locations including:
  • inside the computer power supply
  • along mains extension leads
  • near wall sockets connected to high-draw appliances
A rough population estimate suggests between 18 and 26 individuals were present in the immediate electrical environment. Interestingly, they appeared to show clear preference for alternating current (AC) over low-voltage DC sources, congregating primarily near the 230 V mains supply rather than USB or low-power components.
